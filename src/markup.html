What it actually does The bench does not compile or run your OpMode. It parses the loop into a statement tree — assignments, if/else, and device calls with real arithmetic on gamepad axes — and interprets that tree at 50 Hz against a hardware model. Because it interprets rather than pattern-matches, rising-edge latches, stick mixing and drivetrain maths all behave the way they will on the robot. Where the numbers come from Lever arms, joint axes and the kinematic chain are measured from your STEP assembly. Torque and speed figures are published manufacturer ballparks; the payload and duty sliders let you re-run the sums with your own assumptions, and any lever you've physically changed can be typed straight into the hardware map. Treat the verdicts as a pointer to the joint worth measuring, not a replacement for measuring it. What it can't check Robot Controller configuration names. Nothing in a CAD file can confirm those — they still have to match your Driver Station config by hand, including case.
